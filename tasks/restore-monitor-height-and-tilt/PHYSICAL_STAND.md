# Build the monitor adjustment stand

This is a bench stand built from stock linear-guide parts and a visible indexing pin, holding a conventional 27-inch VESA monitor. A commercial display stand already uses the height-lock mechanism tested here. Step 3 of the [Neomounts NM-M1700BLACK manufacturer's manual](https://www.neomounts.com/_clientfiles/PDF/Manuals/NM-M1700BLACK_Nmanual.pdf#page=3) shows a spring plunger to push clear while moving the telescoping support and three holes in which it re-engages, at 1400, 1450 and 1500 mm. The bench version retains press, slide and positive re-engagement at successive slots and uses the dimensions and loads below. The [BenQ XL2411P manual](https://esupportdownload.benq.com/esupport/ESPORTS%20DISPLAY/UserManual/XL2411P/XL2411P_UM_EN_210129104948.pdf#page=15) separately documents how to support a desktop monitor during a release and how to change tilt. We use the Neomounts manual as evidence for indexing, not as a source for the bench stand's counterbalance or desktop dimensions.

## Parts and assembly

Use a rigid worktop and a production VESA 100 × 100 monitor case. The [LG 27GX790A-B](https://www.lg.com/ca_en/monitors/gaming/27gx790a-b/) is the housing model used for the simulated geometry. LG lists 5.1 kg without the stand, so the stock display alone does not give the moving mass of the simulator fixture. For a low-cost physical trial use a decommissioned 27-inch flat monitor of the same exterior width and comparable upper rim profile, then calibrate the moving mass as described below. Keep the original outer case and bezel free of any added handle or tabs. Use a VESA plate and two bearing ears behind the case. Leave the display unpowered and test the fixture with the load safely supported while setting it up.

The [dimensioned lock drawing](height_lock.scad) gives the position and shape of the pin, shelf faces, paddle and guide relative to the foot at reset. The dimensions are in millimetres. Machine the steel rail from one blank or machine the spine and shelves separately and fasten them rigidly together. The pin slides along Y with the shelf centres fixed vertically. Use the drawing and a gauge pin to align the bar before securing the linear carriage.

1. Bolt a 300 × 250 × 6 mm steel base to the worktop. Mount two gusseted 20 × 40 mm aluminum uprights, a 200 mm miniature linear rail and two bearing blocks on the uprights. A 6 mm metal moving plate joins the blocks. Arrange the stops for 112.88 mm of accessible vertical travel. A fixed mark at 56.44 mm below the upper stop defines the middle setting. Keep the rail and its supports out of the path of the display and its top and sides. At the top setting the tilt axis is about 406 mm above the worktop.
2. Use a horizontal 8 mm steel pin in a bushed guide on the moving plate. Fit a round stop collar for 28 mm of retraction travel and a 0.055 N/mm return spring adjusted to approximately zero bias at the out stop. The working range calls for about 1.54 N spring force at full retraction, before sliding friction. Keep the 36 × 14 × 48 mm orange paddle on the **stand**, connected rigidly to the pin through the bracket shown in the packaged asset. The pin and paddle move together. There is no actuator other than the hand or gripper pushing the paddle.
3. Bolt the slot bar to the fixed upright. Its three detents are 56.44 mm apart. Machine three 12.0 mm high open slots into the matching edge of the 30 × 22 × 151 mm steel slot bar, square to the pin travel. Locate their centres at 293.12, 349.56 and 406.00 mm above the base datum. Locate the slot entrance so a released pin overlaps it by 10 mm. The fully depressed paddle withdraws the pin beyond the bar. Match the exact positions of the pin, paddle and rail with measurements from the USD at reset. Verify that the pin passes freely in and out with the display supported before attaching the cover.
4. Fit the counterbalance and damper using the mounting table below. The complete moving assembly is 7.347 kg. Add trim ballast to the carriage or VESA bracket to match the simulated mass, keeping all ballast behind the display. Fit positive mechanical carriage stops at both ends of travel before installing the springs.
5. Make the tilt hinge from a horizontal 8 mm shoulder-bolt axis and two metal VESA ears with captive thrust washers and friction liners. Keep the pivot horizontal. Trim ballast on the back so the display's measured centre of mass is within 3 mm of the axis. Add stops at 15° backward and 5° forward from vertical. The robot touches the case. At 0°, 5° and 10° backward, the angle may drift at most 0.5° in 10 s after release unloaded by the hands. Adjust the two preload nuts evenly so measured breakaway torque in both directions is 0.25–0.50 N·m. With a 170 mm measured arm this is a tangential pull of 1.47–2.94 N. The lower target clears the 0.214 N·m worst-case gravity moment for a 7.27 kg display with a 3 mm residual offset. This is a bench holding specification. The dimensionless simulation joint-friction setting is not a measured torque.

## Spring, damper and hinge mounting

Use the same XYZ datum as [the lock drawing](height_lock.scad). The positions below are hardware axes and clevis centres, in mm at the upper setting; each moving anchor translates 56.44 mm down at the middle slot and 112.88 mm at the bottom. The [side and rear assembly drawing](stand_hardware.scad) locates the hardware and shows the travel clearances. The stock guide bears side and tilt loads. The spring strips and damper stay on parallel vertical axes behind the guide, and the hinge and pin springs keep their own horizontal axes.

| Component | Fixed hardware and mount | Moving hardware and adjustment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Counterbalance strips | Two mirrored cassettes centred at X = −152, Y = ±60. Pin one wide and one narrow drum in each cassette, coaxial along Y, with the strip exit tangencies at Z = 465. Suggested catalog parts per cassette are Lee `LCF02512075S` (5.94 lbf) and `LCF02506038S` (1.48 lbf). Use independent drums sized to the manufacturer's values. | Bolt the two tails of each cassette to a common rear angle ledge with centre Z = 340, one strip on each side of the cassette centre. Clamp the tails to keep this span at nominal 125–238 mm. Keep at least the initial rated deflection and stay below the maximum working deflection. Measure and select the four strips together for a plateau of 64–67 N. |
| Linear trim spring | An upper M5 screw eye on a 79 mm vertical adjustment slot at X = −152, Y = +25, Z = 390–469. Slots allow preload adjustment without relocating the spring axis horizontally. | Lower eye at the same X/Y and Z = 340. Specify 0.055 N/mm effective spring rate, hook-to-hook free length about 30 mm, and at least 210 mm rated working extension and 12 N rated working load with a captive safety cable. Two equal 0.11 N/mm springs in series are an equivalent if the package fits and each has enough extension. Set the threaded eye to produce `72.07 N − measured strip pull` at the middle height, including initial tension. Confirm the spring remains in tension at the top. |
| Height damper | Mount a 150 mm stroke, 20 mm bore double-acting `MAL20X150-CA` or equivalent cylinder vertically on the rear frame at X = −202, Y = 0. Connect both ports to separate bidirectional needle restrictors vented to atmosphere. Use no air pressure source. The base clevis slot spans Z = 5–95. | Rod clevis at top Z = 395. Measure the assembled cylinder's minimum clevis-centre length `L`. Set fixed Z to `395 − L − 131.44`, leaving 18.56 mm at both stroke ends. If the fitted length places the clevis outside the slot, extend the mounting bracket and keep the same stroke margins. Separate damping from seal friction by pulling at several known speeds in both directions with springs disconnected. Set both restrictors for a 0.8 N change in pull between 10 and 30 mm/s (40 N·s/m target), checked near all three heights. |
| Pin return spring | Bushed guide on the moving carriage, horizontal along Y at X = −86, Z = 406. A captive spring pocket projects toward negative Y; relative to the fully engaged pin centre, the fixed seat is 82 mm toward negative Y. | On a coaxial pin extension, place the inner seat 26 mm toward negative Y from the fully engaged pin centre. Installed spring length is 56 mm engaged and 28 mm retracted, with at most 0.1 N preload. Specify solid height under 25 mm, rate 0.055 N/mm, and operating load at least 1.65 N. Stops set the 28 mm retraction independently of the spring. |
| Tilt hinge | Bolt the two ears to the moving plate, on the Y-axis pivot line at X = −21.56, Z = 406.00. Locate washer stacks at approximately Y = ±55 clear of the case and tighten their preload locknuts symmetrically. | Fix the VESA plate to the pivot shaft and add rear trim mass or slotted ears as needed to achieve the centre-of-mass tolerance and measured holding specification above. The damper does not drive this axis. |

Cover the strip cassettes and extension spring so a loose tail cannot reach the robot. Secure the whole stand to the worktop. Before coupling the display, hand-cycle the full stroke and confirm the slide stops before any spring or cylinder limit is reached.

## Physical trial checks

- With the monitor supported, depress the paddle and confirm the pin fully clears the rail. Release at the upper and middle marks and verify the pin returns far enough to engage. Record the unloaded force needed at the paddle at 0, 14 and 28 mm and check it against the return spring curve. Use matching sliding bushings and report any additional breakaway load.
- With springs accessible and damping disconnected, measure upward pull at top, middle and bottom before coupling the load. Accept 68.97, 72.07 and 75.18 N respectively, within 1 N at each station. Install the moving mass and verify that the carriage settles near mid-travel with the pin withdrawn and holds each slot when the pin is released. Record both tilt breakaway directions under the specified force-gauge and holding checks.
- Use a ruler relative to the middle height mark, an angle gauge on the case, and a fixed view of the pin return and both hands. The scoring windows and tolerances are in the README. The dimensions and force figures above are calibration targets from the simulated fixture, not readings from a built stand. Update force parameters and revalidate the reference trial if a build departs materially from these targets.
