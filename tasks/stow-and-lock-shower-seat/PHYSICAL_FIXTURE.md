# Rebuildable folding-seat test fixture

The task uses an unloaded bench fixture, with the contact surfaces and moving parts of the reference seat. The drawings and force targets below specify a proposed physical build. They are based on the published USD geometry and mass properties. The reported media are simulation trials; they do not include physical measurements.

## Seat, counterweight and mount

All dimensions are in mm. Work in a coordinate frame attached to the hinge centre at reset. The hinge runs horizontally along X. U is the direction out from the wall and Z is up. The seat starts level at 0° and folds to 90°. The unloaded seat fixture uses a 450 by 430 mm seat deck with a 52 mm outer envelope, flush on the hinge line at the back, and a 32 mm diameter round grab rail. The rail grip is 52 mm long along X. Its centre is at X = −275, U = 230, Z = 51. Drill and bolt the rail's two feet to a metal side extension. The two feet are 58 mm apart measured along X; their bottom ends lie 40 mm below the grip centre. Make the extension from 14 mm steel rod and bolt it directly to the side member of the moving seat frame.

Build a light deck from a thin plywood or aluminum sandwich with flush sealed edges supported on a light rectangular aluminum frame. Use [the assembly drawing](fixture_layout.scad) for the centres and orientations of the trim masses, rail and keeper. For detailed exterior contact surfaces, use the pinned source CAD in `assets/forgecad/` and the deterministic rail and catch builders. The physical design uses a light deck and steel trim masses on the same shaft to put the combined centre of mass close to the hinge. Target approximately 1.05 kg for the unballasted deck, moving side members and rail combined. This is a starting size and mass estimate for sizing the trim weights.

- Use a rigid through shaft supported by two bearings, with stop blocks at horizontal and 90°. Set the shaft centre 495 mm above the robot floor with the seat 70 mm in front of the datum of the wall fixture. Bolt the shaft support and keeper bracket to a braced steel test frame anchored to the bench or floor. A bolted structural panel can replace the concrete backing, with the hinge and keeper attached to the frame rather than unsupported panel skin. Keep the panel edges inside the two rear arms (drawing width 450 mm), the bearings outside the trim blocks, and at least 190 mm of guarded clearance behind and below the hinge for the rear ballast as it rotates. Hand-cycle the seat to check the swept volume before fastening a fixed guard behind the robot-facing panel. Run the exercise with the seat unloaded and keep people out of the sweep during actuation.
- Bolt two small trim weights at U = +330 mm, split equally across the deck width. Mount matching adjustable counterweight arms behind the hinge at U = −130 mm, outside the deck edges, and bolt equal masses to them. Offset the plates along X as needed to avoid the shaft bearings and keeper mount. Add a split steel collar on the shaft to bring the rotating assembly to 5.95 kg. Use captive fasteners and locking nuts on all rotating weights. Keep the decks and all weights rigidly coupled to the same shaft.
- Use `fixture_balance.py` with measured properties of the bare rotating assembly to size the initial masses. For example, a 1.05 kg deck assembly with U centre 0.200 m and hinge inertia 0.064 kg·m² calls for roughly 0.10 kg at the front, 1.61 kg at the rear and 3.19 kg at the hinge. This approximation treats the trim blocks as point masses. Include the inertia of the actual shaft collar and trim masses when adjusting the weights and use the force checks below before operating the rig.
- Fit a low-friction rotary damper to the hinge, targeting 3.0 N·m·s/rad at low speed. Mount it between the fixed bearing support and shaft, without a bias spring on the hinge. With the seat near 45°, estimate this coefficient by pulling slowly in both directions at two different speeds. Gravity depends on angle and is unchanged between passes. If the damper adds an offset holding torque, include it in calibration. Use the recorded physical values when updating the simulation if the operating loads differ materially.

The model keeps the mass and represents the counterbalance by moving the effective centre of mass toward the hinge. The finished physical target is 5.951 kg with its centre 5.83 mm forward of and 0.025 mm below the hinge, and its hinge-axis inertia about 0.1025 kg·m². The nominal gravity moment about the hinge is 0.341 N·m at horizontal, about 3% of the unbalanced source model. The physical weights reproduce this moment by opposing the deck's moment. Recheck the force profile if any weight or the deck changes.

## Keeper and spring

Mount the keeper axle parallel to U at X = +246, U = 36, Z = 365 relative to the seat pivot. Use a shoulder bolt in two rigid brackets with a bushing and captive nut. The bracket body is 24 by 86 by 26 mm. The 75 × 15 × 10 mm keeper finger stands along Z in the open position, its centre 36 mm above its axle. The finger swings inward across the seat edge as it returns, with hard stops at 0° open and −90° nominal return. Reproduce the orange control with a 12 mm diameter arm extending 115 mm toward the robot along the axle and an 18 mm diameter pad that extends 52 mm downward from that end in the open position. The anchor, finger, shaft and pad geometry can also be exported from the deterministic fixture builder. Set the empty keeper's stop at −90° and verify the folded seat arrests the finger in front of the edge at approximately −50° without wedging the seat shut.

Fit a torsion return spring concentric with the axle. Use a nominal rate of 0.0573 N·m/rad (1 mN·m/degree), with the unloaded spring at −90°. As a bench check, the spring alone should oppose opening by 0.045 N·m at −45° and 0.090 N·m at 0°. At the 52 mm pad radius those torques correspond to 0.87 N and 1.73 N when pulled tangentially. Use a light friction damper if needed to approach the model's 0.069 N·m·s/rad keeper damping. The contacting seat edge and mechanical stops carry the retention load. Check retention with the grippers away, then reopen the lever while guarding the seat and verify that it can fall away from the keeper. Keep finger-pinch areas screened from people.

## Measure the lifting profile

Hold the keeper fully open, angle the unloaded deck to each listed position, and attach a 0–10 N force gauge to the centre of the grab rail (radius 235.6 mm). Pull tangentially in the direction of lift. Record the positive upward supporting force in near-static raising and lowering passes at each angle. Use their mean to estimate gravity torque; retain the two readings so friction and speed dependence can be checked separately. Bracket 0° with a slow pass starting just above the lower stop. Repeat at least three times and record the angle and actual setup mass.

| Seat angle from horizontal | Target gravity component of lifting force |
| ---: | ---: |
| 0° | 1.446 N |
| 15° | 1.398 N |
| 30° | 1.255 N |
| 45° | 1.027 N |
| 60° | 0.728 N |
| 75° | 0.380 N |
| 85° | 0.132 N |
| 89° | 0.032 N |

The table is calculated from the mass and centre of mass in the packaged simulation. It is a calibration target rather than a set of physical readings. For comparison, a tangential force of 0.2 N yields about 0.047 N·m of torque at this rail. If the mean curve differs by more than 0.2 N, or the effective inertia, damping, or keeper force differs by more than 20%, adjust the physical rig or update the simulation with the measured values and rerun the reference trial. Compare breakaway and slow pull separately. At 85° the predicted gravity torque is small, so there must also be a keeper-open check that the seat moves downward under gravity and a keeper-closed check that it stays at or above 85° when released. Record the two checks separately and verify the video shows both grippers clear in the final state.
